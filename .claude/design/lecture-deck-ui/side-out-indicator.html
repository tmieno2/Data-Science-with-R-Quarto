Side-by-side — indicating that the right box is the output

Right now the pairing is carried by alignment alone: same row, same top edge. That reads for a reader who already knows the convention and is ambiguous for a student meeting it in week three. Four ways to make it explicit, none of which costs vertical space.

1 · Gutter arrow  →

class(corn_yields_df)
[1] "data.frame"
class(corn_yields_tbl)
[1] "spec_tbl_df" "tbl_df" "tbl" "data.frame"

States the relationship as direction — "this produces that". Repeats per row, so it holds for a chunk of any length. Reads at the back of a room and needs no legend. Costs ~14px of horizontal gutter.

2 · Accent edge on the output

class(corn_yields_df)
[1] "data.frame"
class(corn_yields_tbl)
[1] "spec_tbl_df" "tbl_df" "tbl" "data.frame"

Marks the output as a distinct kind of block rather than naming the relationship. Consistent with the callout system, which also uses a left rule for role. Quietest of the four; weakest at saying "caused by".

3 · The word "output"

class(corn_yields_df)
[1] "data.frame"
class(corn_yields_tbl)
[1] "spec_tbl_df" "tbl_df" "tbl" "data.frame"

Unambiguous — it says the thing. But it repeats on every row, which turns into visual noise on a chunk with four expressions, and 10px type is below the size anything else on the slide uses.

4 · Bracket

class(corn_yields_df)
[1] "data.frame"
class(corn_yields_tbl)
[1] "spec_tbl_df" "tbl_df" "tbl" "data.frame"

A drawn connector rather than a glyph, so it carries no typographic weight and never competes with the code. Subtlest; on a projector at the back of a room a 1px hairline is the first thing to disappear.
